## Executive takeaway

**Key judgment (Moderate confidence):** Enforcement does not change *which* products are covered; the principal change appears to be in the *cost mechanics*. From 2026, importers must **purchase and surrender CBAM certificates annually based on reported emissions**, with certificate prices set quarterly against EU ETS auction clearing prices. The binding 2026 constraint is **supplier emissions-data quality** — buyers whose suppliers cannot defend product-level MRV will pay the default-value premium and face customer audit cost. Anchoring procurement on price alone, without product-level emissions data, is the most expensive posture across plausible scenarios.

## Facts (from sources)

- **Fact:** The CBAM **definitive period** started **2026-01-01**, marking the shift from the transitional reporting phase to enforcement; from this date importers must **purchase and surrender CBAM certificates annually based on reported emissions** ([European Commission — CBAM, Taxation and Customs Union](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en); [ICAP — CBAM transitional phase background](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-takes-effect-transitional-phase); [Coolset — CBAM 2026 timeline](https://www.coolset.com/academy/cbam-timeline-deadlines-phases-what-to-expect-2026)).
- **Fact:** CBAM applies to specified product categories: **cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity, and hydrogen** (Commission CBAM page).
- **Fact:** For 2026 imports the Commission applies a **quarterly CBAM certificate price**, calculated as **volume-weighted EU ETS auction clearing prices**; the first 2026 Q1 price has been published on the Commission's CBAM site (Commission CBAM page).
- **Fact:** On **2026-05-07**, DG TAXUD ran a webinar (the second in a series) for **third-country exporters** to help them master CBAM methodology — indicating ongoing operational support and methodology iteration ([Commission CBAM page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)).
- **Fact:** Industry analysis characterizes 2026 as a year of "reckoning" for European steel given enforcement pressure ([S&P Global, 2026-01-07](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/010726-europes-steel-industry-faces-its-2026-reckoning-with-cbam)); secondary reporting on enforcement framing ([Euronews, 2026-01-01](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/01/01/eus-carbon-border-tax-on-heavy-industry-goods-goes-into-effect-risking-trade-escalation)).

## Assessments

- **Assessment (Moderate confidence):** The dominant 12-month cost driver for an importer appears to be not the certificate price level, but **the spread between the importer's actual product-level emissions data and the relevant default value**. Suppliers that cannot deliver auditable product-level MRV force the importer onto defaults and into the worst end of the certificate-cost distribution.
- **Assessment (Moderate confidence):** Customer-side procurement scrutiny will tighten faster than enforcement penalties; large buyers will increasingly disqualify suppliers whose CBAM data does not meet their own reporting standard, regardless of legal compliance.
- **Assessment (Low–Moderate confidence):** Carbon-leakage and trade-friction concerns will produce ongoing methodology adjustments (default values, scope refinements) over 12 months; importers building rigid systems against today's parameters will pay re-tooling costs.

## Assumptions

- The definitive-phase scope is not narrowed within 12 months (no carve-out package).
- The Commission continues quarterly certificate pricing on the announced methodology.
- The importer has, or can acquire, at least basic supplier-side MRV access for top-tonnage SKUs.
- No major upstream-supply disruption (e.g., crisis affecting steel/aluminium imports separately) inside the horizon.

## Actor incentives and leverage

- **European Commission / DG TAXUD:** wants credible enforcement without provoking acute carbon leakage → continues operational support (webinars, default-value refinement) but does not narrow scope.
- **Member-state customs authorities:** want enforceable rules → push for tighter declared-emissions audits as data quality improves.
- **Third-country exporters:** want to retain market access → those who invest in MRV early gain a non-price advantage; those who cannot will lose share.
- **Large EU buyers:** want to reduce their own certificate cost and audit burden → reward suppliers with auditable product-level data; penalize those without.
- **The importer:** wants minimum disruption to existing supplier relationships → temptation to under-invest in MRV until disqualifications start.

## Scenarios (12 months)

1. **Steady enforcement; methodology stabilizes (most likely).** Quarterly certificate prices behave like ETS; defaults tighten gradually; large suppliers professionalize MRV. **Indicators:** Commission default-value updates; sustained webinar program; few public enforcement disputes.
2. **Strict enforcement; default-value tightening.** Commission narrows defaults and intensifies audits on declared product-level emissions. **Indicators:** Commission methodology revisions; named enforcement cases against importers using unverifiable supplier data.
3. **Carbon-leakage carve-out.** Pressure from heavy industry yields temporary scope or threshold relief. **Indicators:** Council debate; Commission communication signaling adjustment; member-state lobbying intensification.
4. **Trade-policy escalation.** Trade-partner retaliation (WTO action, mirror measures) prompts political adjustments. **Indicators:** WTO filings; bilateral trade-policy escalation; coordinated allied responses.

## Options and trade-offs

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Accept default values; pay the premium | Cheapest near-term setup cost | Worst expected cost across scenarios; customer-disqualification risk |
| Build product-level MRV with current top suppliers; outsource verification | Reasonable balance | Ongoing audit and validation cost |
| Restructure supplier panel toward MRV-capable producers | Genuine resilience asset; competitive procurement edge | Re-qualification cost; transition risk |
| Layered approach: MRV-first on top tonnage SKUs; defaults on low-tonnage tail | Cost-effective at portfolio level | Audit complexity; methodology inconsistency risk |
| Pass certificate cost through customer contracts via index-linking | Margin protection | Customer pushback; renegotiation friction |

## Watch-next indicators

- Commission CBAM portal: quarterly certificate prices, default-value updates, scope updates.
- DG TAXUD webinar series (operational signals about methodology iteration).
- Member-state customs enforcement notices and audit communications.
- Customer-side procurement criteria: requests for product-level emissions data and supplier disqualification reports.
- WTO disputes or trade-partner retaliation announcements.
- ETS auction clearing prices (input to certificate pricing).

## Confidence and key unknowns

- Confidence: **Moderate.** Enforcement architecture and product scope are firmly source-backed. Specific default-value trajectories and certificate-price levels at later quarters are uncertain.
- Key unknowns: pace of default-value tightening; speed of supplier MRV maturity; trade-policy retaliation impact on scope and timing.

## What would change the judgment

- A material narrowing of CBAM scope (collapses planning to scenario 3; reduces MRV urgency).
- A named enforcement case against an importer relying on unverifiable supplier data (raises scenario 2 weight; accelerates Option 3).
- A WTO dispute or retaliatory measure that materially shifts political appetite (scenario 4 weight rises).
- Sharp ETS price moves (changes the absolute certificate cost; does not change the relative MRV-vs-defaults logic).
