## Executive takeaway

**Key judgment (Moderate confidence):** The dominant short-horizon risk is not the headline Brent print; it is **policy reversibility**. A ceasefire that holds returns prices toward the World Bank baseline range; a breakdown leaves them in the upside scenario for a quarter or more. Procurement that anchors to today's spot will mis-time both directions. The right structural move is to **shorten cover, build optionality, and pre-authorize hedge triggers** rather than to commit to a price level.

## Assessments

- **Assessment (Moderate confidence):** For a European industrial buyer, gas and power exposure is correlated with crude through LNG-pricing arbitrage but is not 1:1; the binding cost driver in the next 90 days is **regional gas spread volatility**, not Brent level alone.
- **Assessment (Moderate confidence):** A Brent-bias hedge built around the *current* spot would underperform a structural hedge built around the **range** ($86 baseline ↔ $115 upside) because the World Bank scenario is not a forecast but a corridor.
- **Assessment (Low–Moderate confidence):** Customer-side price-pass-through capacity is asymmetric: industries with index-linked supply contracts will compress margins faster than industries with fixed-price commitments.

## Assumptions

- The World Bank methodology and corridor remain a reasonable framing absent a regime-changing event in the next 90 days.
- The buyer has standard energy-procurement infrastructure (basic ability to execute swaps, options, and inventory adjustments).
- No bank-side or counterparty-side disruption to hedging access materializes (no extraordinary margin calls, no broker withdrawal from energy commodities).

## Actor incentives and leverage

- **Major exporters (OPEC+ producers, US shale operators):** want price stability above baseline → likely to under-supply the market in the upside scenario, over-supply only if prices threaten demand destruction.
- **Importing-country governments (EU, Japan, Korea):** want price ceilings → may release strategic reserves; effect on price is real but bounded.
- **Iran and US negotiators:** each want a deal that preserves face; their incentive structure makes oscillation likely, not a clean direction.
- **The buyer:** wants margin protection more than it wants a price-call win → values **defensible procurement discipline** more than directional bets.

## Scenarios (90 days)

1. **Ceasefire holds; market reprices toward baseline (modal).** Brent drifts toward the upper end of the World Bank baseline ($86/bbl) but does not collapse. **Indicators:** sustained ceasefire announcements; resumed tanker traffic through Hormuz; OPEC+ communications about supply stabilization.
2. **Oscillating tension; range-bound at $95–$115.** No durable resolution; prices stay in the upside corridor. **Indicators:** repeated incident-followed-by-de-escalation news cycles; regular tanker-insurance premium moves.
3. **Breakdown; durable supply gap.** Closure or sustained disruption forces sustained prices above $115 with non-trivial gas spillover. **Indicators:** named incident causing prolonged closure; major reroute commitments; insurer withdrawal from regional shipping.
4. **Unrelated demand shock.** Recession or large policy shift compresses demand. **Indicators:** EU/US PMI deterioration; sustained inventory builds in OECD storage.

## Options and trade-offs

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Hold current cover, monitor | Lowest near-term cost | Most exposed in scenario 2/3 |
| Shorten contract tenors; build inventory at the lower end of the price range | Cheap optionality | Working-capital cost; storage operational risk |
| Layer pre-authorized hedge triggers (pull-the-trigger conditions written before the next move) | Procedurally disciplined; removes panic decisions | Requires up-front governance work |
| Restructure key supply contracts to cap-and-floor structures | Caps both sides of the corridor | Counterparty cost; renegotiation friction |
| Reduce energy-intensive output for 30–60 days | Robust to scenarios 2 and 3 | Customer-impact; margin loss in scenarios 1 and 4 |

- TTF / NBP gas spread relative to Brent (correlation often breaks under regional events).
- Strategic reserve release announcements (US SPR, IEA member countries).

## Confidence and key unknowns

- Confidence: **Moderate.** Price levels are well-sourced; the World Bank corridor is well-documented; the choice between scenarios over 90 days is the dominant unknown.
- Key unknowns: durability of ceasefire; willingness of major exporters to use spare capacity; regional-gas-spread response if the upside scenario persists.

## What would change the judgment

- Durable ceasefire with tanker-traffic restoration to pre-event levels (collapses planning to scenario 1).
- A new named closure event or hostile incident affecting tanker traffic for >7 days (scenario 3 → primary).
- An IEA/World Bank revision moving the baseline above $95/bbl (the corridor shifts; recalibrate).
- Sustained TTF/NBP decoupling from Brent (gas-specific hedge rebuild required).
