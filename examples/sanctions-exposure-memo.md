## Executive takeaway

**Key judgment (Moderate confidence):** Material exposure is concentrated in (a) dual-use SKUs that may fall under tightening export-control lists and (b) financial rails through correspondent banks rather than in general commercial activity. The dominant uncertainty is enforcement posture, not legal text.

## Assumptions

- Current sanctions architecture remains broadly stable for 12 months — no novel regime change.
- The firm has standard EU dual-use compliance but limited regional KYC depth on partners.
- No live updates from OFAC, EU, or UK lists were checked for this memo.

## Facts vs assessments

- **Fact:** EU dual-use export controls apply extraterritorially to listed items.
- **Fact:** US secondary-sanctions risk attaches to non-US persons supporting designated entities.
- **Assessment:** Banking partners — not the firm directly — are the primary transmission channel for secondary risk in this corridor.
- **Assessment:** Re-export risk concentrates around a small subset of SKUs, not the full catalogue.
- **Assumption:** Local distributor due diligence is functioning but not stress-tested.

## Actor incentives and leverage

- **Local banks:** want to preserve correspondent relationships → cooperative on enhanced KYC.
- **Regional regulators:** want optionality → ambiguous public posture.
- **Designating authorities (US/EU):** want signaling effect → likely to act on visible re-export corridors before broad sectoral measures.
- **The firm:** wants predictability → values continuity over upside.

## Scenarios

1. **Stable enforcement (most likely).** Narrow, named designations. Exposure stays localized. Cost: modest compliance uplift.
2. **Targeted enforcement against re-export corridors.** Specific intermediaries named. Cost: partner switching, customer disruption for 1–2 quarters.
3. **Broad financial-rail action.** Correspondent access narrows. Cost: payment restructuring, possibly retreat from one market.

## Options and trade-offs

| Option | Pros | Cons | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold and harden (deeper partner KYC, dual-use ring-fence) | Preserves position, proportionate cost | Does not protect against scenario 3 | [inference] |
| Restructure payment rails through compliant hub | Reduces banking-channel risk | Operational lift, slower receivables | [analyst-judgment] |
| Contract footprint to lowest-risk segments | Robust to all scenarios | Forfeits market position, hard to reverse | [analyst-judgment] |

## Watch-next indicators

- New OFAC or EU designations naming regional banks or distributors.
- Tightening of dual-use thresholds covering core SKUs.
- Partner-side KYC escalation (request volume, document depth).
- Public enforcement action against a comparable peer firm.
- Correspondent-bank communication on enhanced due diligence requests.

## Confidence and key unknowns

- Confidence: **Moderate**, because the framework is stable but enforcement priorities are not directly observed in this memo.
- Key unknowns: planned designations; specific partner-bank correspondent posture; pending dual-use list amendments.

## What would change the judgment

- Direct enforcement action against a peer firm in the same corridor.
- New dual-use list additions covering core SKUs.
- Withdrawal or restriction of correspondent banking relationships in-region.

## Limitations

This memo is reasoning-only and illustrative. It is not legal, sanctions, or compliance advice. Real decisions require counsel, current designations review, and partner-specific diligence.
