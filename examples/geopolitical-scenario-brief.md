## Baseline

The current direction of travel — narrowing access at the leading edge, friend-shoring of advanced fab capacity, broadening of end-use controls — continues with periodic incremental tightening.

## Scenarios

### S1 — Steady tightening (most likely)

- Continued incremental list updates and end-use controls; no regime rupture.
- **Triggers:** new node-class additions; EUV-adjacent tooling restrictions.
- **Implications:** compliance overhead grows; long-tail Chinese demand for legacy nodes persists.
- **Indicators:** quarterly entity-list cadence; new license-required end-uses.

### S2 — Bifurcation

- Two largely separate semiconductor stacks emerge; multinational firms choose a side per product line.
- **Triggers:** export-control extraterritoriality dispute; major reciprocal Chinese measures on critical inputs.
- **Implications:** customer-by-customer architecture decisions; loss of fungibility across regions.
- **Indicators:** Chinese export controls on rare-earth or gallium-class inputs; allied alignment on enforcement.

### S3 — Escalation shock

- Sudden broadening of controls following a security event; abrupt loss of market access.
- **Triggers:** acute Taiwan-related contingency; major tech-transfer enforcement case.
- **Implications:** discontinuous revenue impact; forced product redesign.
- **Indicators:** sharp rhetoric escalation; emergency-rule export-control actions.

### S4 — Selective de-escalation (least likely, not impossible)

- Narrow carve-outs for specific civilian categories.
- **Triggers:** broader diplomatic settlement; commercial pressure from allied industry.
- **Implications:** marginal relief, not strategic reversal.
- **Indicators:** general-license activity; bilateral working-group output.

## Most decision-relevant takeaway

The firm should architect for **S1 with optionality for S2**, not optimize for S4. Specifically: avoid product designs that depend on single-jurisdiction supplier substitution being legal in three years.

## Watch-next indicators (cross-scenario)

- Cadence and scope of US entity-list updates.
- Allied alignment (EU, JP, KR, NL) on enforcement.
- Chinese countermeasures on critical inputs.
- Investment/subsidy posture for advanced packaging in allied jurisdictions.

## Confidence and key unknowns

- Confidence: **Moderate** on S1 dominance over 24 months; **Low** on relative weight of S2 vs S3.
- Unknowns: Taiwan contingency probability; allied tolerance for extraterritorial enforcement.

## What would change the judgment

- A major reciprocal Chinese measure on critical inputs (raises S2 probability).
- A Taiwan-related contingency (collapses planning into S3).
- A bilateral framework agreement (raises S4, but only narrowly).
