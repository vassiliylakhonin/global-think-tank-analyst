## 4. Draft Memo

### Decision

Whether the fintech should approve expansion as planned, restrict onboarding to
lower-risk customer segments, or require enhanced due diligence for Caspian- and
UAE-linked trade flows.

### Bottom Line

The expansion risk is not "Central Asia exposure" in general. The risk
concentrates where four channels overlap: SME exporter opacity, Caspian routing,
UAE-linked counterparties, and payment rails that create correspondent-banking
pressure. The safer posture is conditional expansion with segment-level
enhanced due diligence rather than a blanket block or unrestricted launch.

### Key Assumptions

- The fintech is onboarding SMEs, not large state-owned exporters.
- Customers may trade goods with dual-use, sanctions-adjacent, or hard-to-classify
  characteristics.
- Some flows involve Caspian-connected logistics and UAE-linked counterparties.
- No live sanctions, company, ownership, or transaction screening has been
  performed in this packet.

### Primary Driver

Primary driver is: the combination of weak customer transparency and cross-border
routing optionality, not any single jurisdiction.

### Mechanism

Risk transmits through:

- **Customer onboarding:** SMEs may have incomplete ownership, trade-license,
  UBO, or beneficial-control documentation.
- **Goods classification:** invoices and HS-code descriptions can understate
  dual-use or sanctions-adjacent characteristics.
- **Routing:** Caspian and adjacent routes can be legitimate trade corridors, but
  they also increase ambiguity when goods, counterparties, or end-users are not
  clearly documented.
- **Payment rails:** correspondent banks and payment providers may de-risk if
  they see repeated exposure to high-risk routes, opaque counterparties, or UAE
  intermediaries without clear commercial rationale.
- **Counterparty adjacency:** UAE-linked trade partners are not inherently high
  risk, but they can add screening and rationale burden when combined with
  Iran-adjacent, Russia-adjacent, or re-export-sensitive flows.

### Exposure Map

| Exposure area | Why it matters | Risk control question |
|---|---|---|
| SME exporters with incomplete UBO data | Ownership opacity can hide sanctioned-party adjacency. | Can the fintech verify beneficial ownership and control before onboarding? |
| Caspian-connected routes | Routing can obscure final destination or end-use. | Is the route commercially justified and documented? |
| UAE-linked counterparties | Legitimate hub activity can overlap with re-export risk. | Is the counterparty screened and commercially explainable? |
| Dual-use or hard-to-classify goods | Misclassification can create sanctions/export-control exposure. | Is goods classification reviewed for high-risk categories? |
| Correspondent-bank tolerance | Bank partners may impose tighter rules than formal law requires. | Would a tier-1 correspondent accept the flow if reviewed? |

### Scenarios

| Scenario | Trigger | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled expansion | Customer data, goods descriptions and route rationale are complete. | Expand with enhanced monitoring for specified segments. |
| Segment restriction | High-risk goods or route/counterparty combinations appear repeatedly. | Restrict onboarding for those segments until enhanced controls are in place. |
| Correspondent-bank pressure | Banking partner asks for explanations or rejects flows. | Pause affected corridors and review risk appetite. |
| Enforcement shock | New sanctions or export-control action names a relevant route, bank, commodity, or intermediary type. | Move from monitoring to immediate re-screening and escalation. |

### Watch-Next Indicators

- New OFAC, EU, UK, UN, or national designations involving regional banks,
  logistics companies, trading houses, or intermediaries.
- BIS / EU export-control updates affecting goods categories used by SME
  exporters.
- FATF / EAG / national AML updates changing risk posture for relevant
  jurisdictions.
- Correspondent-bank requests for route, counterparty, or goods explanations.
- Customer concentration in a narrow set of UAE-linked counterparties.
- Increase in route changes without commercial explanation.

### Recommended Posture

Use conditional expansion:

- approve low-risk segments with standard onboarding;
- require enhanced due diligence for Caspian-connected trade flows, UAE-linked
  counterparties, unclear goods classifications, or incomplete ownership data;
- predefine escalation triggers for correspondent-bank concerns, sanctions-list
  updates, goods-classification ambiguity, and route anomalies;
- maintain a clear audit trail separating verified facts from analyst judgment.

### Confidence

Confidence: Moderate for the structure of the risk mechanism. Low for any
claim about current sanctions, bank policy, counterparties, routes, or goods
without live verification.
