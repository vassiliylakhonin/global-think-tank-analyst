## Signal
The Federal Reserve faces a structurally harder policy environment than the post-2022 rate cycle: tariff-driven goods inflation is pushing prices up while trade-policy uncertainty and higher input costs are pulling growth down. The standard playbook — raise rates to fight inflation, cut rates to support growth — does not resolve cleanly when both pressures are operating simultaneously. Corporate planning assumptions anchored on a smooth Fed pivot carry significant downside.

**EVIDENCE ACCESS LIMITED: no live verification performed in this environment.**

## Why it matters
- Tariff-induced inflation is supply-side, not demand-side. Rate hikes cannot fix a supply disruption and can actively worsen the growth component of the stagflation pressure.
- Companies that assumed a Fed easing cycle in 2026 and priced floating-rate debt, M&A financing, or investment plans accordingly face a timing mismatch if cuts are delayed.
- The ECB signal (2026-04-30) showed a central bank holding despite growth concerns; the Fed faces the same tension with a larger domestic inflation shock from tariffs.

## Decision question
For a CFO or corporate treasury managing floating-rate debt, capital expenditure timing, or FX hedging with USD exposure: how much policy uncertainty should be priced into the rate path, and what is the cost of being wrong in each direction?

## Quick assessment
- **Fact (structural):** Tariff-induced inflation is not directly controllable by Fed rate policy; the transmission mechanism runs through demand suppression, which hurts growth before it reduces tariff-driven price levels.
- **Assessment:** A prolonged pause — not a cut cycle — is the most defensible base case for corporate planning. Verify current Fed guidance before acting.
- **Assumption:** No disinflationary shock resolves the constraint inside 6 months.
- **Unknown:** Fed's internal weighting between mandates under simultaneous pressure; actual tariff pass-through velocity to consumer prices.
- **Main risk:** Planning around a rate-cut cycle that arrives later, or shallower, than assumed.

## What to watch
- FOMC statements and dot-plot revisions: watch for explicit language on tariff inflation vs. demand inflation.
- Core PCE and CPI prints excluding food and energy: tariff pass-through speed determines how long the Fed stays constrained.
- Labor market data: softening employment alongside sticky inflation is the canonical stagflation signal.
- ECB and Bank of England rate decisions: divergence from the Fed creates FX and funding-cost implications.
- US Treasury yield curve shape: inversion deepening or flattening signals market expectations.
- Political commentary on Fed independence: any credible pressure is a signal to stress-test the disinflationary scenario.

## What would change the judgment
- A tariff reversal or comprehensive trade framework → disinflationary surprise scenario; rate-cut path opens faster.
- Core PCE sustainably below 2.5% for two consecutive prints → Fed gains room to cut.
- Sharp labor-market deterioration (unemployment rising >0.5pp in a quarter) → Fed pivots to growth mandate; cuts begin faster but signal deeper stress.
- Explicit Fed guidance naming tariff inflation as "transitory" → markets re-price cuts quickly.
