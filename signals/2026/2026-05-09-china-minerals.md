## Signal
China **suspended** certain export-control measures on critical minerals — **rare earths, gallium, germanium, antimony, graphite, superhard materials** — from **November 7, 2025 until November 10, 2026**, pending bilateral negotiation ([Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/china-suspends-export-controls-certain-critical-minerals-related-items.html); [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/china-hits-pause-on-rare-earth-export-controls-and-what-it-means-for-supply-chains/); [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/china-suspends-export-prohibition-on-superhard-materials-us/)). Stricter end-user/end-use reviews for dual-use graphite to the US are also paused. Separately, on **2026-01-01**, China's Export Licensing Catalogue **added** controls on rare-earth compounds (samarium, gadolinium, lutetium), silver, and other materials. Extraterritorial enforcement under the October 2025 measures remains delayed until November 2026.

## Why it matters
- The suspension is **time-bound**, not a policy reset. The architecture remains in place and is reinstatement-ready.
- Six months is enough to **build inventory or close a non-China supplier qualification cycle**, but not enough to reshape midstream processing dependence.
- A plan that depends on the suspension being extended is the most regret-likely posture.

## Decision question
For a US/EU mid-market manufacturer using rare earths, gallium, germanium, antimony, or graphite, what is the highest-value use of the 6-month window — inventory build, non-China qualification, customer-contract restructure, or some combination?

## Quick assessment
- **Fact (from sources):** Suspension 2025-11-07 → 2026-11-10 covering rare earths, gallium, germanium, antimony, graphite, superhard materials; 2026-01-01 catalogue update added rare-earth compounds and silver (Pillsbury, Clark Hill, Fastmarkets).
- **Assessment:** Treat the window as procurement runway; reinstatement on tighter terms is the central downside scenario.
- **Assumption:** Suspension runs to its stated horizon without earlier rescission.
- **Unknown:** Which subset (if any) China reinstates first if bilateral conditions deteriorate.
- **Main risk:** Customer-side disqualification of suppliers without auditable non-China qualification records if reinstatement returns abruptly.

## What to watch
- MOFCOM announcements amending or rescinding the suspension; export-license issuance pace.
- US BIS / EU dual-use list updates during the window (allied tightening can offset Chinese loosening).
- Spot prices and bid-ask widening on gallium, germanium, antimony, graphite, rare-earth oxides.
- Customer due-diligence escalation: requests for non-China qualification records.
- China customs export volumes for the listed items.

## What would change the judgment
- Any specific MOFCOM reinstatement, even narrow, before November 2026 → planning to "build inventory + accelerate qualification" becomes urgent.
- A bilateral framework agreement extending the suspension → reduces inventory-build urgency.
- Allied dual-use tightening on a class the firm depends on → Chinese loosening becomes irrelevant for that class.
